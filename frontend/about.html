Not another listing app.
A transparency engine for the mandi system.

KisanLink was built for SIH 2026, Problem Statement 26033: "multiple intermediaries reduce farmers' earnings and increase consumer prices." Most solutions to that problem stop at "connect farmer to buyer." KisanLink goes further — every number on this site is computed from real data, not assumed.

What's actually novel here

📊

Real data, not a mockup

16 years (2010–2025) of daily Maharashtra mandi prices across 20 commodities — 100,907 records sourced from Agmarknet via CEDA (Ashoka University). Most student prototypes for this problem statement use invented sample numbers; every price, forecast, and benchmark here traces back to a cited source.

🎯

Forecasting that reports its own accuracy

Both ARIMA and seasonal SARIMA are grid-searched per commodity and evaluated on a genuine 6-month holdout — whichever generalizes better is kept, and its holdout MAPE is shown, not hidden. Mango's forecast is flagged as unreliable rather than smoothed over, because a model that admits its limits is more useful than one that doesn't.

🔍

Stakeholder-level price transparency

Beyond a single "farmer vs. consumer" split, Tomato/Onion/Potato get the full RBI "TOP" study breakdown — farmer, trader, wholesaler, and retailer share of the consumer rupee — visible to farmers before they price their listing, not buried in a report.

🚚

Logistics math, not a hardcoded number

Every listing is matched to its nearest of 7 real collection hubs by haversine distance. Open listings heading to the same hub are then grouped into one consolidated pickup route (greedy nearest-neighbor) and compared against everyone traveling independently — the distance-saved percentage is computed fresh from real coordinates every time, never a fixed marketing number.

🗣️

An assistant that speaks the farmer's language

An optional Claude-powered chat answers questions grounded in the app's live listings and forecast data — and Sarvam AI adds voice input/output in Indian languages, so a farmer can ask "onion ka bhaav kya hai" out loud instead of navigating menus.

🤝

Honest about its own limits

The savings calculator uses RBI's published Terms of Trade study, not a live retail feed, because that data source was down during development — and this app says so plainly instead of quietly pretending otherwise. Trust is a feature.

Honest limitations

  • Mango's ARIMA/SARIMA forecast has a high error rate — flagged, not hidden — because its price series is unusually volatile in ways a simple time-series model doesn't capture well.
  • The savings calculator uses RBI's published research benchmarks rather than a live retail price feed, since that source was unavailable during development.
  • This is a functional prototype (SQLite, single server) — a production deployment would need authentication, payments, a real logistics partner integration, and a managed database.
